# Monthly performance report — PETL — 2026-10

**The month in one paragraph.** PETL spent $653,851 on Meta in the 30 days to October 26, the most it has ever spent in a 30-day stretch, and got back $1,070,861 in Meta-attributed revenue at a 1.64 ROAS and a $78.18 cost per purchase (verified, Meta Ads MCP, pulled 2026-10-27). That's 17% more spend than the prior 30 days for 7% more purchases, so the extra money bought sales at a worse rate. ROAS has now slid five months in a row on a calendar basis, from 1.92 in May to 1.61 in October so far, against a 2.2 Meta goal. The story underneath is not "the ads got worse." It's that the account kept buying the same expensive audience, women 55 and up, with video at $170 to $500 per thousand impressions, while the one cheap lane it found this month, a static three-device image from the in-house team, bought Facebook feed at $43 per thousand and lifted the last week to a 1.79 ROAS. NSY's newest batch, launched October 16, didn't carry its weight. The two biggest concepts in the account are both NSY's (PETL60 and the PETL65 "Top 3 Chargers for Grandparents" idea), but both run near 1.6 to 1.7, and the PETL65 family is clearly tiring. November brings Black Friday on the 27th, three new PETL 2 Plus colours on the 4th, and higher auction prices, so the next month has to be built around cheap reach and offer creative rather than more of the same video.

**How this read was built.** I pulled account totals for every calendar month from May 1 to October 26, then each week from July 29 to October 26, then the last 30 days against the prior 30 days at account, campaign, ad set and ad level. For the three biggest ads I pulled age, gender and placement splits, and I pulled the primary text and headline for the 14 biggest creatives. I mapped ad names to NSY concept codes using the naming rule in the digest: "NSY | PETLnn" is an NSY concept, "INTL" is PETL's in-house team, and the token after INTL is the in-house creator or editor (Sean, CLD or Claire, AR, ROG). Then I read every number against the calendar: the August 3 price rise, the September 1 PETL 2 Plus price rise, Rakesh's October 2 "we are taking you over" message, the October 9 creation of the TOP PERFORMERS ad set, the October 15 to 17 consolidation and the 40 to 50 ads loaded on October 16, and the record revenue weekend of October 24 and 25. The 30-day window covers about 96% of spend in the ads I tallied by hand.

## What worked

**INTL | CLD_1001 | V2 | Three-device ecosystem** (in-house, static, THREE DEVICES, created Oct 8). $53,583 in the window at 1.80 ROAS, 739 purchases, $72.51 per purchase, $47.25 CPM, 1.13 million impressions, frequency 1.55, outbound CTR 1.90% (verified). Of that, $53,189 landed in the last seven days alone. 66% of its spend ran on Facebook feed at a $43 CPM and 1.92 ROAS (verified, placement breakdown). **The pattern.** Three things carried the month. The first is cheap reach. The CLD_1001 static did something no video in the account did: it bought Facebook feed at a fraction of everyone else's price and found buyers outside the core 55+ female audience, at a ROAS above the account average. `ad-account-analysis.md` makes the point that statics usually lean toward the bottom of the funnel, so some of that is warm demand being captured. But a 1.55 frequency and a reach this big say it was also reaching new people (inferred). The Andromeda lens in `andromeda-v2.md` explains why: a format change is the strongest kind of difference Meta recognizes, and a clean product static sits in a different group from the talking-head videos that fill PRODUCT FOCUS, so it got fresh delivery and its own cheaper auctions (inferred). The PETL Analytics brief for the week of Oct 26 had already flagged it: "THREE DEVICES 2.29× on 9.8% of spend — 'winner starved, needs more ads'" (stated, Gmail). In the final week the account fed it, and the week's ROAS jumped.

The second is offer clarity at the bottom of the funnel. Every bundle static beat the account average, and PETL61 works partly because it sells an upgrade to people who already know PETL. That fits the durable pattern in 03-performance-learnings, "Price/bundle framing converts BOFU." Per `ad-account-analysis.md`, those ads are doing a different job from a cold video, so they shouldn't be crowned the best ads outright. They are the most efficient, not the growth engine.

The third is durability. PETL60 has run since September 4 and is still the biggest single concept. Under the top-spender rule in `ad-account-analysis.md`, Meta's spend is its prediction of where the most results are, so PETL60 is a working ad even at 1.69. It's NSY's main asset in the account this month (inferred).

**Why it underperformed.** The losing ads share one trait: they get the click and lose the sale. PETL125 at 5.09% CTR, PETL72 at 5.00%, Sean_911 H3 at 5.15%, INTL | SEAN915 | V4 at 6.01% and 0.84 ROAS, and PETL105 at 4.49% all sit at or near the bottom on cost per purchase (verified). The digest's own read of the 90-day table says the same, "High CTR ≠ efficiency" (inferred there, confirmed again here). The likely reason is that these hooks are curiosity or nostalgia hooks for older viewers who click easily, and then the $64.95 price on the page is the first real filter. Price was 47% of "what almost stopped you" answers in the January to March survey, which predates the price rise (stated, N-R3), so the gap is probably wider now (inferred). `killer-performance-ads.md` puts it as wanting "relevant attention," the attention of people who will buy, and a hook that doesn't qualify the buyer for a premium price spends money on people who won't pay it.

PETL125 is also an awareness-stage mismatch. It is built for problem-unaware viewers ("Broad 35-65 problem-unaware," Notion PETL125), but the external research reads this as a highly aware, claim-saturated category, Schwartz stage 4 to 5, where buyers already know power banks exist (inferred, external-research-2026-10.md). A long story lead that takes 60 years to reach the product is a hard sell to people who already know the category and mostly need a reason to pay more for this one.

The Top 3 family's slide is fatigue, not failure. It was the account's engine from August 11 and drove ~35% of purchases at its peak (stated, Rakesh's Oct 5 readout). The in-house re-shoot extended its life, but Andromeda's entity logic says a same-script re-shoot with a similar older woman talking to camera adds little new reach (inferred, `andromeda-v2.md`, Tier 3 and the "different creators, similar appearance" example). The relaunches of PETL48 and PETL25 failed for a related reason: they came back into an account where the same buyers had seen them for months, at a price $15 higher than when they won.

## What we tested

No formal slate with success criteria was locked at the start of the month. That is itself a finding: the month's bets were set by three overlapping sources, the Round 8 plan (Sep 29), Rakesh's six recommended tests (Oct 5), and the hypotheses written into each Batch 7 brief. Here's what actually got a read.

**Test: a capped scaling ad set for proven ads (TOP PERFORMERS, created Oct 9).**
- Baseline: one ad eating 42% of spend ($56K) at 1.59 (stated, Fathom 2026-10-09).
- What ran: PETL60 V1 and Sean_911 H4 duplicated into a new ad set.
- Result: $100,252 at 1.69 and $78.88 per purchase, CPM $334.51, frequency 1.29 (verified). PETL60's copy ran 1.72 there, against 1.64 in its old home.

The idea was to protect the account from one ad eating everything while keeping winners spending. It slightly beat the account average, but it didn't change the shape of the account: the two ads inside are the same two concepts that were already the biggest, now with new ad IDs. Per `andromeda-v2.md`, a duplicate of the same creative into a new ad set is not a new ad to Meta. It shares the same entity and the same audience, which is consistent with that ad set's $335 CPM, the highest in the account (inferred). Verdict: inconclusive on whether it helped, clear that it didn't diversify.

**Test: Rakesh's recommended tests from the Oct 5 readout.**
- Of the six, only "Grandparent-as-recipient gift UGC" got a live read, through PETL73: $10,146 at 1.57 (verified). The rest (60s to 70s male UGC, product demo plus 30% bundle badge for women 65+, a "3 chargers vs 1" demo for men 55 to 64, an older couple buying two, an older female unboxing with all three devices in the first 3 seconds) have no ad in the account yet that I can match by name (data-limited; Notion statuses for PETL118, PETL119 and others show "Edit Approved, Need to Launch," stated).

The grandparent-as-recipient read came in under the account average at a $255 CPM. It points at the same expensive audience as the Top 3 family. Inconclusive at $10K.

**Test: price-objection statics (PETL106, PETL108, PETL112).**
- PETL108 Price Announcement: $1,731 at 1.89 (verified). PETL112 Minimal Price: $3,991 at 1.43, paused (verified). PETL106 Yes, It's Pricey: launched per Notion, spend under the threshold I pulled (data-limited).

The hypothesis across these briefs was that price is "now the main objection" after the move to $64.95 (stated, Notion PETL96/98/106). The early read splits by how the price is framed. A plain dollar-off line lost. A price announcement with a bundle price ($64.95 struck, $45.47 per unit on the 5-pack, per the brief) did better on small money. That fits the lifetime pattern that bundle framing converts and discount-only hooks don't (stated, 03-performance-learnings). Inconclusive on volume. Worth noting: PETL108 carries a "REMAINING: 171/1000" stock line, which the digest flags as unverified (data-limited).

**Test: new formats and personas from Batch 7.**
- PETL125 problem-unaware AI VO timeline: failed on efficiency (1.32 on $31.6K).

## What we learned

**The account isn't short of good ads so much as it's short of cheap reach.** This is the main lesson of the month. The core buyer the account has leaned on all year, women 55 and up who own an iPhone, Watch and AirPods, is the right buyer, but Meta now charges a lot to reach her. PETL60's TOP PERFORMERS copy paid $410 per thousand impressions, Sean_911 H4 paid $171, and PRODUCT FOCUS as a whole paid $159 (verified). The CLD_1001 static paid $47 and still returned 1.80. When the account finally put money behind that static, the whole week's CPM fell from $136 to $76 and ROAS rose from 1.52 to 1.79 (verified). Reach for the 30 days grew 37% while frequency fell, which says the account found new people this month for the first time in a while (verified numbers, inferred meaning). Charley's line that the account was "spending about 4x what we were in May but reaching roughly the same number of people each week" (stated, Slack 2026-10-20) described the problem; the last week showed one way out. Per `andromeda-v2.md`, that kind of fresh delivery comes from real format and visual difference, not from more versions of the same thing.

**Price changed which creative works, and the account hasn't fully caught up.** Since the August 3 price rise, revenue per purchase rose from about $82 to $129, and the ads that won at $49 have mostly stopped winning. PETL48, PETL25 and PETL65 all scaled when the price was lower and all faded or failed on relaunch (verified trajectory, inferred cause). What's held up since the rise is creative that pre-sells the value or the deal: bundle statics at 1.86, PETL61's upgrade framing at 1.96, the PETL84 long-copy founder note at about 1.97 over its three versions (verified, though PETL Analytics flagged all PETL84 variants as declining in the week to Oct 18, stated). The creative that lost is the kind that earns a cheap click and leaves the price for the page. Half of orders are still a single unit at around $96 order value (stated, Charley, Slack 2026-10-20), and the single-unit tier lost $9,634 on 1,361 orders from Sep 1 to 10 while 2-pack and up were profitable (stated, Charley, Slack 2026-09-11). So creative that moves people into a 2-pack or 3-pack is worth more than its ROAS shows (inferred).

**High click-through is a warning sign in this account, not a win.** Six of the ads with a CTR of 4.5% or more had some of the worst cost per purchase in the window (verified). Older viewers on Facebook click on curiosity, nostalgia and gift ideas easily. At $64.95 they don't buy on the same impulse. The month's evidence suggests the hook needs to do some of the qualifying: show the three devices and the product early, signal the price or the bundle, or speak to someone already shopping for a gift. That matches `killer-performance-ads.md` on relevant attention and the account's own durable rule, "Vague, clever or lifestyle creative loses. Be literal and specific." (stated, 03-performance-learnings).

**The words under the ads never change.** All 14 creatives pulled for this report, spread across NSY and in-house, across video and static, across August through October launches, carry the same primary text and headline: "One charger for your iPhone, Apple Watch, and AirPods. Say goodbye to bulky cables & oversized battery packs." / "One Charger. Three Devices." (verified, creatives endpoint). The external research found the same thing in the Ad Library and added that "No live ad copy read mentions the 30-day trial" and that Ugreen and The Ridge now run the same one-charger promise (verified, external-research-2026-10.md). Messaging is the weakest lever for Meta's grouping (Tier 4 in `andromeda-v2.md`), but it's the main place a buyer reads the offer, the trial and the reason to pay more. Right now that space says the same thing on every ad. Price is the top objection, and the account's strongest unspent answer to it, "try it risk-free for 30 days" (confirmed accurate by Grace, 2026-09-25, stated), appears in none of the copy.

**Credit and origin are getting tangled, and it matters for how NSY is judged.** The account's biggest ad family this month started as NSY's PETL65, and its biggest single ad is the in-house re-shoot, which is credited to INTL in the client's dashboard (inferred from ad names and the digest). PETL60 is NSY's and is the single largest concept by spend. NSY-labelled ads took 48% of spend. Yet the client's dashboard shows NSY at a 10% hit rate against 16% for CLD (stated, PETL Analytics). The data supports a fairer read than "in-house is beating NSY," but it also says plainly that NSY's newest work this month, the Oct 16 batch at 1.40, was weaker than in-house's newest work, CLD_1001 at 1.80 and Sean_1016 "URGENTOFFER" at 2.27 on $3,908 (verified). Both things are true.

## What we should do next month and why

**Put real weight behind the cheap-reach lane, with ads that are different, not copies.** The evidence for this is the strongest thing in the report: one static at a $43 Facebook feed CPM moved the whole account's week. November will make impressions more expensive ("Media costs rise ahead of the season," stated, `gifting-and-q4-creative.md`), so the account needs more ads that can buy cheap feed reach. That doc also warns against adding a pile of new personas going into peak: "Depth on a known buyer beats breadth across speculative ones when media is expensive and the window is short." So the move is not to chase new audiences. It's to reach the proven buyers, the 55+ Apple owner and the person gifting to them, through a cheaper format than talking-head video, and to lean into the one extra buyer the sales data already proves, men buying Space Black. NSY's November statics should lead with the product and the three devices in the first glance, be built for Facebook feed, and be spread across visibly different people and settings so Meta treats them as new ads rather than versions of CLD_1001. PETL Analytics already points the same way: "Direct the next brief round into Gifting and Proof and comparison … with a genuinely different buyer/scene, not another Three-device variant" (stated, Gmail daily 2026-10-26). Good starting points from the brief queue and the October data: the Space Black buyer, who runs "91% men at 1.84x (strongest men 45-54 at 4.1x)" (stated, PA weekly), a male-led static set to match CLD_1001's better-than-average returns from men 45 to 64 (verified), and the three-generations colour carousel for the Nov 4 launch (stated, Grace's brief for week of Oct 26).

**Build Black Friday around bundles and the trial, starting now.** Black Friday is November 27 and its brief was due October 23 and is marked not done (stated, PA weekly). The month's clearest efficiency came from bundle statics at 1.86 and the history says occasion-themed offers win: Christmas and holiday creative ran 2.67 and bundle or save ran 2.63 in the lifetime export (stated, 03-performance-learnings). The client's own plan is to lean on the 30% off 5-pack, with a pre-Black Friday test two weeks early and a fallback to about 10% off (stated, Fathom 2026-09-25). Two things have to be true before any of it ships: the client confirms the exact offer and the free-shipping rule (the $129 threshold versus Charley's Oct 20 ask for "a free shipping threshold," stated), and every claim passes the claim-risk table in the external research, especially "risk-free for 30 days" over "no questions asked." The order of work: themed bundle statics first because they're fastest and proven, then a pre-Black Friday video that leads with gifting and the bundle price, then the risk-free trial set (PETL111, PETL118, PETL119) as the first real test of the trial as a lead message. That last one is the month's biggest untested idea, and it speaks straight to the price objection.

**Replace the tiring Top 3 family with format changes, not another re-shoot.** The family took 21% of spend at 1.56 and is fading. Under `andromeda-v2.md`, the right move on a fading winner is a Tier 1 or Tier 2 change, a new format or a new vehicle, not the same script with a similar creator. The concepts already built for that are the two-person tier list (PETL126, "Dad, Rate These Chargers"), the 50+ PSA yapper (PETL127), and the ranked static (PETL81). Ship those first, and judge them on whether they reach new people at a lower CPM, not just on ROAS in week one. Keep PETL60 and PETL61 running. They are the account's top spenders, and `ad-account-analysis.md` is blunt that killing a top spender on CPA usually shrinks the account rather than freeing money. But PETL60's hook rests on Black being back in stock, a July event (stated, digest), so its claim is getting stale. The natural refresh is the November 4 colour launch: the same apology-and-demand structure, with a true new event behind it.

**Stop the leaks and fix the copy layer.** Three small moves cost nothing creative. Turn off the Instagram followers traffic campaign, which spent $7,999 at 0.17 this month against a standing rule (verified, stated rule). Stop duplicating the same creative into new ad sets as a scaling move, since it adds spend at a $335 CPM without adding a new ad (inferred). And start testing primary text. Every ad says the same words today, so the first test is cheap: two or three primary texts on the next batch of statics, one carrying the trial, one carrying the bundle math, one carrying "the one you actually carry" line the research flags as PETL's longest-running organic post (verified, external research). That doesn't create new delivery on its own, but it puts the offer and the risk reversal where the buyer reads.

**Measure November honestly.** Q4 will swing every number. `gifting-and-q4-creative.md` says the months before peak should be read on leading signals, not ROAS, because a shift toward the gift buyer takes Meta time to relearn and that stretch is "expensive and inefficient by design." Expect ROAS to dip the week before Black Friday as CPMs rise, then spike on the sale days. Read the month by blended MER against the 2.4 goal and the "2.1 is okay" floor, by new reach and frequency, and by whether NSY's new concepts take a fair share of spend, rather than by any single day (inferred). Because this is the first monthly report, every recommendation above starts its lineage here. The November report should say which ones shipped and what they did.

## Open loops

**1. The cheap-reach static found buyers the videos don't reach.**
- Observation: CLD_1001 bought Facebook feed at a $43 CPM and drew a younger, more male mix (35 to 54 heavy, men about 45%, men 45 to 64 at 1.84 to 1.87), while the grandparent videos pay $170 to $500 per thousand to reach women 55 and up (verified, Meta breakdowns, Sep 27 to Oct 26).
- Pull: Surprise. The account's whole year has been built on older women, and the month's most efficient scaled ad sold to a different crowd at a quarter of the price.
- Question: Who are the people buying from the three-device static that the grandparent videos never reach?
- Justification: If there's a sizable buyer group outside women 55+, it changes who NSY casts, what settings it films in, and where the Q4 budget can grow without paying the top CPM.
- Territory: Personas.

**2. Order value jumped, but we don't know what the ads are actually selling.**
- Observation: Meta revenue per purchase rose from $73 in May to $129 in October (verified), while Charley says about half of orders are a single unit at around $96 (stated, Slack 2026-10-20) and the single-unit tier lost money in early September (stated, Slack 2026-09-11).
- Pull: Tension. A $129 average and a $96 single-unit order can't both describe the typical buyer, so the mix underneath matters.
- Question: What share of the account's purchases are single PETL units, bundles, and PETL 2 Plus, and which ads drive each?
- Justification: If certain concepts push buyers into 2 and 3 packs, those concepts are worth more than their ROAS shows, and Black Friday creative should be built on them.
- Territory: Product. Routes to the brand, since the order mix lives in Shopify.

**3. The ads older viewers click most are the ones they buy least.**
- Observation: PETL125 (5.09% CTR, $99.71 per purchase), PETL72 (5.00%, $120.58), Sean_911 H3 (5.15%, $120.06), SEAN915 (6.01%, $130.48) and PETL105 (4.49%, $95.31) sit at the bottom on cost per purchase (verified, Sep 27 to Oct 26). The same pattern shows in the 90-day table (stated, digest).
- Pull: Pattern. It keeps showing up across NSY and in-house ads, video and static, and across months.
- Question: What happens between the click and the checkout that stops these high-click viewers from buying?
- Justification: If the drop is price shock on the page, hooks need to qualify for price; if it's something on the page itself, the fix sits outside the creative.
- Territory: Messaging.

**4. Price is the top objection, but the risk-free trial never appears in the ads.**
- Observation: Price was 47% of "what almost stopped you" survey answers before the price rise (stated, N-R3), every pulled creative carries the same copy (verified), and the external research found no live ad copy mentioning the 30-day trial (verified, 2026-10-27). The trial concepts PETL111, PETL118 and PETL119 are approved but not launched (stated).
- Pull: Gap. The most direct answer to the top objection exists on the site and in the briefs and has never been put in front of buyers.
- Question: What signs exist today that buyers who click and leave are held back by the fear of wasting $64.95 rather than by the price itself?
- Justification: If the fear of a bad buy is the real block, the trial becomes the lead message for Q4, not a closing line.
- Territory: Messaging.

**5. The re-shoot outspent the original.**
- Observation: In-house Sean_911 H4, a re-shoot of NSY's PETL65 idea, took $107,801 in the window while PETL65 V4 itself fell to $8,653 (verified). Sean_911 H3 of the same idea faded to $1,801 (verified).
- Pull: Curiosity. The same idea in three cuts got three very different verdicts from Meta, and the reason isn't visible from names.
- Question: What is different about the Sean_911 H4 version that made Meta pick it over the original and over H3?
- Justification: Knowing whether it's the person, the first frame or the edit tells NSY what to change when it rebuilds a fading winner, which is the main job for November.
- Territory: Creators and talent.

**6. The client's scoreboard and the account's spend tell different stories about NSY.**
- Observation: NSY-labelled ads took about 48% of spend at 1.62 and in-house about 46% at 1.71 in the window (inferred tally from verified data), while the client's dashboard shows NSY at a 10% hit rate against 16% for CLD (stated, PA weekly) and Rakesh said 75% of sales came from INTL in one week (stated, Slack 2026-10-02). The biggest INTL ad is a re-shoot of an NSY concept.
- Pull: Tension. Both can't be the full story of who is driving the account.
- Question: How does Rakesh's analytics decide whether an ad counts as a hit, and who gets credit when one team's concept is re-shot by the other?
- Justification: That definition decides how NSY's work is judged going into Q4 and whether NSY should be building concepts for in-house to re-shoot or finished ads of its own.
- Territory: Messaging (the volume layer: how many winners each side is finding). Routes to the brand.
