# Persona voice and emotion library — PETL

## Purpose and how to use

This is the no-invention companion for writing PETL copy. Every line in quotes below was written by a real buyer or commenter. Pick the emotion you want the ad to land, read the evidence for it, then check which persona it belongs to in `personas-profile.md` before you write. These are starting points, not finished lines. Run them through the claims and voice checks in the two VoC docs before they go in an ad.

One thing to hold the whole time. PETL's buyers are mostly 55 and older, mostly women (verified, Meta 90-day pull, 75.9% of purchases from 55+). Their language is plain, short, and calm. The panic, "1%," and "lifesaver" language in PETL's own scripts is the brand talking, not the buyer.

## How to read the evidence tags

- **Source tags.** `SURVEY #GPxxxx Qn date` is the post-purchase survey, by Shopify order number and question. Q2 is "What are you hoping to get out of this product?", Q3 is "Why did you choose to buy from us compared to a different brand?", Q4 is "What almost stopped you from buying today?". `WEB-REVIEW date` is the January website review export. `FB-COMMENT name, age` is Grace's April capture, first name and last initial only. `JUDGEME date` is a recent PDP review.
- **Observed** means the customer names the feeling outright: "love," "anxiety," "hate," "worry." **Inferred** means the feeling is clear from the words and stakes but not named.
- **Persona tags** use the slugs in `personas-profile.md`: `no-fuss-family-keeper`, `apple-design-loyalist`, `team-provider`. Tags on single quotes are inferred.

## Dataset summary

778 free-text survey answers from 836 respondents are the spine. They came in January to March 2026, when one PETL cost $49, so they are the voice of the early buyer before the price rise to $64.95. The survey is the most emotionally useful source by volume, but each answer is short, so most emotion is inferred. The January reviews and the 5 October Judge.me reviews are the most openly emotional ("I freaking love it"), but there are only 16 with text. The Facebook comments are the richest source of doubt and almost no feeling beyond it. Missing: the long reviews, the unhappy reviews, Reddit, and anything with a buyer's age attached.

## Persona reference

- `no-fuss-family-keeper` — flagship. The older Apple household keeper, mostly a woman 55+, who wants charging to stop being a chore for her and her people.
- `apple-design-loyalist` — secondary. The Apple owner who buys the best-made, best-looking option; leans male and 45 to 64, inferred.
- `team-provider` — emerging. Buys in numbers for a team or practice. No verbatims exist yet.

## Top emotional engines

Ranked by my judgment across frequency, how close the language sits to the purchase, how many source types carry it, and how useful it is for creative. There is no scoring formula behind it.

### Emotion 1 — Relief from the cord mess

**Felt sense.** The small, daily weight of too many cords, pucks, and chargers, and the lift of having one thing instead.

**Frequency.** Wire, cord, and cable words in 44 of 778 survey answers. Bulky, heavy, clunky, cumbersome in 9 more. 30 of 320 Q2 hopes name fewer cables (classification read). Also in January reviews and FB comments. Three source types.

**Intensity.** Medium-low. It is irritation, not pain. But it is the most consistent feeling in the corpus and it is concrete, which makes it easy to show.

**Journey.** Trigger and exploration. It is what she is fed up with before the ad, and what the demo resolves.

**Persona alignment.** Mostly `no-fuss-family-keeper`; the Design Loyalist feels it as clutter on the nightstand or desk.

**Contextual triggers.** Packing for a trip, the nightstand, the purse, the "tech bag."

**Verbatim evidence.**

### Emotion 2 — Being ready, for me and mine

**Felt sense.** The quiet comfort of knowing there is always power on hand, so she does not have to think about it.

**Frequency.** Security, worry, emergency, and backup words in 13 of 320 Q2 hopes. "On the go" phrasing is common in Q2 as well (classification read). Survey only. Mixed.

**Intensity.** Low to medium. Calm, protective, not frightened.

**Journey.** Exploration and evaluation. It is the benefit she is buying, more than the problem she is fleeing.

**Persona alignment.** `no-fuss-family-keeper` above all.

**Contextual triggers.** Travel, being away from home, emergencies, a spouse or kid with a dead phone.

### Emotion 4 — Guarded hope at the moment of buying

**Felt sense.** "I'm taking a chance on you, please don't let me down." Hope with one hand on the wallet.

**Frequency.** Hope, hoping, hopefully, trust, faith, and chance words appear across Q2, Q3, and Q4. 15 of 320 Q2 hopes are about the product simply working as advertised. 23 of 222 Q4 answers doubt legitimacy (classification read). **Intensity.** Medium, and the closest emotion to the purchase in the corpus, because these people were literally at checkout.

**Persona notes.** This is not a hook emotion. It is an evaluation and post-purchase emotion. Answer it with proof and the trial, and use it in follow-up email. A testimonial that opens "I wasn't sure it was legit either" speaks right to it.

### Emotion 5 — Burned before

**Felt sense.** Annoyance and wariness from a power bank that let them down, and the wish that this one finally works.

**Frequency.** 4 Q3 reasons name a failed prior pack, plus the hopes and objections that echo it (classification read). FB comments add heat and "corded" complaints. Competitor reviews carry it loudly. Thin by count, high by intensity.

**Intensity.** High. The most charged language in the corpus.

**Journey.** Trigger, when the old pack fails, and evaluation, when they compare.

**Persona alignment.** Both. Strongest for `apple-design-loyalist`, who has owned other packs.

**Verbatim evidence.**

## Low-confidence signals

- **Pride in giving.** Fewer than ten records: "Very cute and worthy for gifts to my family and friends" (#GP4882), "Gifted !" (#GP5175 Q2), "Sleek charger and a gift to a friend" (#GP5074 Q2), "My husband coming into the room just before I submitted my order." (#GP7459 Q4), and "Received one for Christmas 🎄 And absolutely LUV IT❣" from the receiving side. Thin because the survey window had no holiday. A post-Christmas survey would settle it.
- **Money strain.** The price objection is the biggest single doubt (89 of 222 Q4 answers), but the emotion under it is rarely named. Where it is, it reads as real strain, not haggling: "I don’t really have money to spend" (#GP8389, 2026-03-26), "Low on funds" (#GP7602), "The funds or lack there of" (#GP5110), "Nothing really just making sure I’m had enough money" (#GP6188). These belong to the objection doc. Do not turn them into guilt in creative.
- **Indignity of being stuck by an outlet.** The team's PETL74 brief uses "I've sat by a plug socket in a coffee shop waiting for my own phone to come back to life. At seventy-one." That is a written script line, not a customer quote (stated, 02-audience doc). No buyer in the sources says it. Unsupported as customer emotion.
- **Panic.** "Anxiety" appears once among buyers (#GP7126), about carrying the right cords, not about a dead phone. Dead and dying words appear 4 times. The brand's panic engine has almost no customer evidence.

## Creative language opportunities

1. **"Freedom from Cords" and "Less cables."** The most repeated feeling, said in the plainest words. The brand says "Cable-Free, Clutter-Free Charging." The customer says "Not having to deal with so many wires." Use hers.
2. **"For me & family."** The household instinct is in the customer's words, not only in the PDP bundle names. A static or hook built on "Portable charger for me & family" fits the flagship persona exactly.
3. **"The puck sized powerhouse."** The one customer-made metaphor in the corpus. Worth a static headline as a quoted review.
4. **The legit check, answered out loud.** Nobody in the account's top 30 ads is briefed to answer "is this real." The customers hand you the opening: "Wasn’t sure if this was going to be a legit product." (#GP6117).
5. **The linked story in order #GP4104.** Q2 "A viable charger for my wife’s Apple Watch when we travel!", Q3 "I tried another brand I found on Instagram but it didn’t work.", Q4 "That prior failure…". One buyer, three answers, a whole arc: a husband looking after his wife's Watch on trips, burned once, trying again. Use as a story seed, not a quote to put in someone else's mouth.
6. **"Easy," said over and over.** "Easy charging," "Easy to use and easy to carry," "Looks easy." It is not clever, and it is what the older buyer wants to hear.

## Contradictions and warnings

- **The brand's emotional story is panic; the buyer's is ease.** Website copy: "Never get caught at 1% again." Winning scripts: "Tried to pull up my boarding pass at the gate and my phone was dead. I never want to go through that panic again." Buyers: 4 of 778 answers mention a dead phone. The "Low Battery Anxiety" angle ran 1.29 ROAS (stated, N-R3).
- **Several "customer" lines in the project files are brand copy.** "Pure minimalist genius" is the website's own testimonial. "It's a lifesaver," "It lives in my bag," and "no more rummaging through drawers looking for the right cable" are ad script lines. The 02-audience doc lists them under "Real customer language." Do not use them as customer proof.
- **The 02-audience doc paraphrases some real quotes.** Its Carmencita line, "I love it — charges your phone, watch, and AirPods and it's easy to carry," does not match the export. The exact review text is "I like it, easy to charge my phone, waych and airbuds." (WEB-REVIEW 2026-01-13). The reviewer names in the export also sit between reviews, so who wrote which line is ambiguous. Credit reviews by date, not name.
- **"Minimalist" and "premium" are the brand's words.** One buyer says "Minimalist." (#GP7868) and one says "super portable and very minimal." (#GP7895). Nobody says premium.
- **The persona profile's flagship is older, but no quote here carries an age.** Every persona tag on a quote is inferred.

## Open loops

1. **Gift emotion is missing.** Gifting is a big part of the PETL story and the Q4 plan, but the survey window held no holiday and fewer than ten records carry any giving feeling. *Pull: Gap.* **Question:** What do PETL buyers say they feel when they give a PETL as a gift? *Justification:* Q4 creative is being built on gifting emotions nobody has heard a buyer say. Territory: messaging. Routes to the brand, through a post-holiday survey question.

2. **Calm versus panic.** Buyers describe calm readiness and cord relief; the brand writes panic. *Pull: Tension.* **Question:** Why does the brand's dead-phone panic language barely show up when buyers explain why they bought? *Justification:* If the older buyer is put off by panic, the hook register for the flagship persona should change. Territory: messaging.

3. **Same word, two people.** "Design" carries two meanings in the corpus: "cute" and "pretty" from some buyers, "not plastic" and "engineering" from others. *Pull: Pattern.* **Question:** Which buyers say "cute" and which say "not plastic"? *Justification:* If it splits by gender or age, statics and casting should split with it. Territory: messaging.
