# 04 — Writing Rules (scripts, hooks, overlays, statics)

These come straight from Grace's corrections across eight rounds. Where she said it in her own words, the quote is included. Read the matching section before writing anything.

---

## A. The bar

- **10/10 or it isn't done.** "I think the copy right now is like a 4 out of 10. We want it to be a 10 out of 10." A low score means rebuild, not polish.
- **Read the specs before ideating.** Accuracy beats cleverness (Month 1: concepts that implied things PETL can't do were scrapped).
- **Solve a big, felt problem.** A whole concept on circular vs rectangular was "a waste of creative". Circular is a supporting USP, not the problem.
- **Scenarios must be realistic and frequent.** "If your phone dies right as your Uber pulls up, I think most people would still get in it." Concerts are too rare; use weekly moments (checkout, commute, coffee, the gate).
- **Put yourself in the avatar's shoes. Go below the surface** (see `02`). "This is very surface-level stuff... we need to go deeper within that iceberg."
- **Reuse proven language.** "This was a strong hook that worked, so getting a man to do that exact hook, why not." Swipe winners closely (Dojo).

## B. Register — spoken, not written

- PETL65 is the benchmark for older-demographic UGC: short declarative sentences, plain "and/but" constructions, honest concessions, about half the runtime on features, proof and close.
- "Think and write like how a human naturally speaks." "If a creator was to say this line, would it sound realistic?"
- Concessions stated flat: "It works, but then you're tied to the wall." "It holds a lot of charge, I'll give it that."
- Plain literal phrasing. "Who says, 'Then the phone would go'? Say, 'And none of that mattered until I left the house. Then the phone would die on me.'"
- **American English.** No mum, stood, sat, quid, bloke, plug socket, fortnight, "proper" as an intensifier, "loads of", "spot on". Say mom, outlet, guy.

### Banned AI tells
- **Time-stamped symptoms:** "dead by 2pm", "30% by lunch", "dying by 3pm". "I hate the whole 'dying by lunchtime, dying by 2 pm, 3 pm'... dead giveaway that it's AI."
- Three-fragment runs ("Always charged. Always ready. Always there.")
- Narrating curiosity: "here's the thing", "here's what nobody tells you", "let that sink in"
- Self-aware literary lines, "if I'm honest" asides, overly polished "written" phrasing
- **Em dashes and spaced hyphens** in scripts ("you had a lot of dashes in the scripts nowadays, which I don't like. It just makes it look AI-generated")
- "for example" ("can you stop saying 'for example'?")
- Greeting-card lines and brand-speak: "peace of mind", "because you can't be there", "power, perfected"
- The "Banned — The Definitive Guide" PDF in `context/` (Ben's checklist; the `de-ai-ify` skill) is a useful extra pass for AI clichés. Its erotica/fiction sections don't apply.

### Word choices
- "**Snap it on**" (not "stick it on", "take it out and stick it on")
- "**iPhone**", not "phone", when describing compatibility
- Name the product plainly for older viewers: "this little circular power bank", "this tiny charger", "this viral little power bank". **Never describe PETL as a "thing" or a "circle"** (a circle could be anything). Some older approved scripts in `07` break this; fix it when you reuse them.
- **American spelling:** aluminum, color, favorite. (Some swipe-file originals use British spelling.)
- "**Apple Watch**", never "iWatch"
- "about another ten hours" (talk time), not "charges up to 10 hours"
- "almost the size of a golf ball", "smaller than your palm", "barely bigger than a powder compact", "fits in your coin pocket"

## C. Hooks — ~80% of a concept's potential. Spend the effort here.

**The Round 6 (PETL70–78) standard. Write every UGC hook set like this** (the examples below are Americanized from the originals, which slipped into British phrasing):
- A **specific moment in the creator's own voice**, with real numbers and a real age where relevant. Example: "I was standing at the gate with my boarding pass on my phone and three percent battery."
- **Four distinct entry points** per concept:
  1. Moment / story
  2. Indignity ("I spent forty minutes on an airport floor next to an outlet, at sixty-one, guarding my own phone.")
  3. Frequency / authority ("I fly to see my grandchildren every single month.")
  4. Direct-address callout ("If your boarding pass lives on your phone...", "Grandparents...", "iPhone users 50+...")
- **Hooks build over 3–4 sentences, with natural self-correction** where it fits: "My grandma hates every gift I get her. Well, she doesn't say that. She says thank you..."
- **State the problem and tease the solution without naming it:** "I got her this little gift that was under seventy dollars, and now it never dies when she's out."
- **Hook 1 must explain WHY** (why mom was buying a new iPhone), or it "seems like every other video".
- "Hack" beats "check": "people are more inclined to listen when it sounds like a hack... some sort of secret that's about to be revealed." Time promises work ("in about thirty seconds").
- Strong words stop the scroll ("hates", "embarrassing").
- **Name all three devices (iPhone, AirPods, Apple Watch) in hooks for older audiences.**
- **Mention Apple** where it fits (as praise or comparison). It lifts attention.
- **Proven openers to reuse:** "Nobody tells you this when you get an Apple Watch / an iPhone..." · "I can't believe they don't sell this at the Apple store." · "My wife told me I had to get this." · "I asked ChatGPT for the best MagSafe power bank..." · "I'm 58 and not a tech person, but..."
- **Winner formula (Grace):** "If you need to buy a gift for your grandparents or your parents, here's the best gift you can buy for under seventy dollars." That's conditional targeting + superlative + price anchor + withheld identity.
- Gifting hooks stay **evergreen**: "a birthday or a holiday coming up", not Father's Day or Black Friday, unless the round is seasonal.
- Hooks must be **filmable by one creator** (a "2021... 2023..." timeline hook isn't).
- For AI-ranking hooks, vary the AI: one ChatGPT, one Claude, one generic "asked AI instead of Googling".
- **Rejected styles:** clipped fragments ("It's 3pm. You're on 12%. Again."), "Raise your hand if your charger owns you", fragmented timestamps ("8:14 AM"), vague questions ("What do you get someone who buys whatever they need themselves?" became "What do you buy a doctor? They earn well, they buy whatever they want...").

### Hook staging
- **Hook 1 is full frame.**
- **Only ONE alt hook is split screen** (problem footage on top, creator on the bottom). The others are B-roll cutaways + talking head. Don't split-screen all three alts.

## D. Text overlays

- "Text overlays are very important, and they're just as important as the visual hook itself... We need to think as if the viewer is going to be viewing the video with the audio muted."
- **Two short lines max.** Line 1 = callout or problem; line 2 = payoff or promise. Example: "Forever hunting for your iPhone charger?" / "Here's the fix, under $70."
- They **complement the spoken hook, never repeat it**, and they must stand alone as an argument sound-off.
- "The text overlay needs to leave some sort of curiosity." Tease a problem you're about to solve. "1 setting, $1,200 saved" failed because it has no context. "Before you spend $1,200 on a new iPhone, watch this" worked.
- Caps and size-ups for emphasis work ("My grandma HATES every gift I get her...").
- The overlay must fit the concept (if the hook is about a gift that fixed a problem, say so).
- **Body overlays are few.** Many rows are marked "No overlay". Captions carry the dialogue. Overlays only highlight key features. "You have a text overlay for literally every single line."
- **Standard body overlays:** "iPhone | Apple Watch | AirPods" · "~10 extra hours" · "80,000+ customers | 1,191 reviews"
- **End card:** "Up to 30% off | 30 days risk-free | 1-year warranty | gopetl.com" (add "iPhone 12+" if useful)
- Never put a contested stat on screen alone (e.g. "30% faster"). Never show the full price next to "20% off".

## E. Body structure (UGC)

**Hook → Agitate 1–3 → Tried and Failed → Discovery → Reveal → Features → Transformation → Proof → CTA**

- **Agitate** — don't rush it. Peak on an **indignity beat with a beat of silence** (no overlay, no music swell).
- **Tried and Failed** — usually the big bulky power bank that stays in the drawer ("It's heavy, it's the size of a brick, it needs a cable, so I never once took it out with me").
- **Discovery** — a gift from a daughter/granddaughter/wife works well for older avatars. "My heart sank a bit. I thought, oh, another thing I'll have to learn."
- **Reveal ("the turn")** — land the **magnetic click**. "There's nothing to learn. It attaches to the back of your iPhone with magnets."
- **Features in depth** — "5 lines approx talking about the product won't work." Cover:
  - world's first circular
  - snaps on, self-aligns, no cords
  - all three devices
  - stand / nightstand
  - aluminum and glass, "feels like something Apple would make"
  - battery widget, no app (plus a short free-app line)
  - colours (golds, Plus) where relevant
  - packaging = lovely gift
- **Transformation** — stay on her face.
- **Proof** — 80,000+ customers, 1,191 reviews, sold out 7 times (brand-level), how long she's had it.
- **CTA** — recipients + reason + offer + risk reversal. Example: "Buy three and the shipping's free. It comes with a full one-year warranty, and if you don't love it you've got 30 days to send it back."
- **"That's not even everything"** — after a comparison, stack extra PETL benefits.
- **Use-case line that works:** "On a long flight when there's no outlet. Commuting to and from work. Or just lounging at home on the couch, so you're not stuck tethered to a wall."
- Selling **PETL 2 Plus: never trash the original** (it's the volume seller and people re-buy). Plus = more battery for heavy users; mention big phones (Pro Max) once at most. Frame it as "The PETL you love, upgraded".
- **Length:** UGC winners run 90–120s. Ignore 30–45s on concept cards when they conflict.
- The **Apple honesty disarm** works: "I'm not saying it's an Apple product. It's not. It's compatible with Apple products. But honestly? It's the MagSafe battery pack Apple should have made."
- Ranking scripts: PETL gets **9/10 with a small flaw** ("I wish it came in more colors"), or 10/10 when a launch justifies it (PETL63). Concede Anker's capacity.

### Creator notes (in the brief)
- Tell creators to **improvise and use their real age and details**. Editors caption what the creator actually says.
- **Change location every 2–3 lines** (within one home if it's a home shoot). Exception: a seated tech-reviewer unboxing, where busy hands keep engagement.
- **Yapper format:** the creator is always doing something (cutting a bright, unusual fruit like dragon fruit or pineapple, which is Grace's favorite and does well on TikTok, or doing makeup / lip gloss in a parked car; this yapper setup is the one exception to the "no parked-car POV" rule). Never a static talking head against a blank wall. About 90/10 talking head to B-roll; B-roll only around the PETL reveal.
- **Corporate scripts:** office-style setting (home office is fine), creator in a blazer. B-roll = the creator using PETL in practical scenarios. Team/office shots come from stock or AI (creators can't film colleagues). No custom logos.
- Cinematic mode on iPhone gives the premium feel that worked on the Amazon vs PETL winner.

## F. Video visual direction (Editor Notes column)

- **Brief fragments, a couple per row.** Example: "Grandma frustrated, tapping phone. Close-up: phone dead. PETL snapping on." Editors given room do better than ones following detailed AI-written instructions.
- **Concrete visual direction only:** specific B-roll, clip/footage direction, on-screen text, caption/hook overlays, text-card contents. Direct and literal ("Cut to: hand snapping PETL onto iPhone, macro").
- **No strategy, compliance or why-notes in Editor Notes.** Those go in Other Editor Notes or the compliance section.
- **Show real faces and the human struggling with the exact problem being spoken about** (agitate it on camera). Never phone-screen-only or device-only hook shots (e.g. a dead Watch screen alone).
- Timeline beats must visually match the line being said.
- Every Apple mention: cut in 3–5s of real Apple pack B-roll.
- Music: **warm, chill lofi, no swell at the reveal** (the Round 6 standard for every PETL UGC script). Earlier rounds (3–5) used trending masculine funk / phonk for male tech-review edits; only use that if Grace asks.
- Captions: dynamic, burned-in, editor's style, readable sound-off. Larger text for 55+.

## G. Static copy

- **Headlines like a nine-figure tabloid copywriter (Daily Mail / Mirror):** concrete nouns, real numbers, specific verifiable claims, direct address, curiosity gap. "If they wrote a headline like this, they would be fired."
- **5–7 words** (up to 7 for comparisons). The visual carries ~80%.
- **No abstractions, greeting-card lines or brand-speak.** Rejected examples: "Designed to belong", "Premium In. Premium Out.", "They'll Keep The Box", "Apple gave up. We didn't.", "Power, refined".
- Good examples: "Stop Buying Your Team Water Bottles", "Your Third One Costs $32", "Their Phone Won't Die At School Again", "Fits in your coin pocket.", "Thinner than 5 credit cards.", "The Gift That Charges Everything", "Can't believe they don't sell this at Apple."
- Headlines in one static should share a vibe (all money-saving, all gifting).
- **Exception:** long-copy founder announcements (Icy Bear "IT'S OFFICIAL" format) follow their own clipped structure: what's ending → what it is and why it's good → why the price is moving. Bold the price, the product name and the core benefit. The tabloid rule doesn't apply.
- **Feature-led beats colour-led.** Colour is the scroll-stop; function callouts sell.
- **Acquisition statics: no price point.** BOFU statics: offer visible, ideally tied to an occasion and themed end to end (colours, props, setting).
- **One trust badge per static** ("breathing room").
- **Never brief:** receipt-style graphics, list-style visuals (packing lists, handwritten lists), price-sticker gimmicks. (Note: the handwritten sticky-note static PETL91 was built in Round 6; the later rule is no list-style or handwritten-list visuals.)
- **Don't recycle static setups** (plain PETL front-on, PETL-on-iPhone hero). Fresh visual concepts each time.
- Some references are copy inspiration only (e.g. HexClad "last pan you'll ever buy"). Then mood, colours and look are fully up to the designer.

## H. Fabricated content — the line

- **Don't fabricate branded AI interface screenshots** (fake ChatGPT/Google/Gemini UI recommending PETL) for paid ads. That's a Meta deceptive-content and FTC endorsement risk on PETL's ad account. Real screen recordings of real queries are fine. So is a generic chat layout that isn't styled as a specific brand.
- **Don't fabricate social proof presented as real customer comments in final production.** Invented comments are for layout placeholders only, clearly marked. In final production use real comments pulled from FB/PDP (first name + last initial) or the PDP's own proof points. This line has held under pressure in past rounds and doesn't shift.
- On comment-bubble statics, Claude builds everything else (layout, headlines, offer, compliance) and marks the comment slots for real comments. For a source of real ones, pull from facebook.com/PETL.Official launch posts (see `02`).
- Testimonial statics styled with name/photo/age/stars must use real reviews. Otherwise write them as unattributed brand copy.
