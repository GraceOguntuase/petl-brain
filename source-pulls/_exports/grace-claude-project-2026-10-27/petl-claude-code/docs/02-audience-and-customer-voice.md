### What this means when you write
- **Default older-audience scripts to grandma/women**, not grandpa/men (majority of the older audience is female).
- **Keep briefing some younger creators.** The client wants more younger creators and new avatars tested (younger males and females, new angles, winning formats with new avatars), even though 55+ is the core buyer.
- The older demo is **not tech-savvy**, and many don't know the word "MagSafe". Name the product plainly in hooks ("this little circular power bank", "this tiny charger") and vary the descriptor.
- They value **function over style**, hassle reduction over specs. They reject price-first pitches and read pure scarcity as manipulative.
- **Testimonials work better for women. Tech reviews and comparisons work better for men** (Grace).
- Women care more about aesthetics; middle-aged men care about "the best charger with the most battery" (Grace).
- Older audiences skip technical fast-cut edits ("they're more male-oriented").
- **Older buyers buy in bulk** for the household (Rakesh). They lock in savings in one go, and that drives 5-pack AOV.
- **Spousal dynamic:** the woman often buys for her husband, and men lean on their wife's recommendation ("My wife told me I had to get this" was a proven hook). Most women in their 40s–50s are buying for husbands, not dads.
- Grandparents "really like to invest in their iPhone accessories."
- **Mentioning Apple in hooks increases attention** (as praise or comparison, never as an attack).
- **Coded settings:** coffee shop reads male, grocery checkout reads female. Golf is niche, so keep references simple so younger buyers and women gifting can follow.
- Apple Watch dying hooks have done well for older audiences. Apple Watch charging is what reviews talk about most (Rakesh).
- Audience is **American.** No British words or phrasing.
- Avoid settings that have stopped working: driver's-seat / parked-car POV (overused), grandkid's game / bleachers (didn't perform). Favour settings relatable to women 75+.

### Note on the Dojo playbook (Oct 2026)
The Dojo playbook says PETL is "a younger tech audience, so probably more open to AI formats". **That conflicts with the account data** (55+, female 65+). Treat AI-format tests (skeletons, Pixar stories) as one test per batch at most, and skew them to formats older viewers follow.

## Psychology Grace wants (go below the surface)

- "This is very surface-level stuff... we need to go deeper within that iceberg." The dead battery is the surface. Find the identity or relationship stake underneath:
  - **Doctor/nurse gifting**: being noticed; she looks after everyone else's family and can't reach her own.
  - **Game day organiser**: identity as the one who planned it; the phone dies and suddenly "you're the problem."
  - **Night out organiser**: self-sufficiency; the organiser ends up dependent.
  - **Older female driver**: independence; the kids offering to drive her.
  - **Grandparent**: "She was rationing her own phone" (no photos of the grandkids, wouldn't ring unless it was important).
  - **65+ testimonial**: indignity ("I've sat by a plug socket in a coffee shop waiting for my own phone to come back to life. At seventy-one." PETL74. Write "outlet" in future; "plug socket" is British).
- Relief for 65+ is about **reduction** (fewer cables, one place), not power: "it's not that I've got more charge. It's that I've stopped worrying about it."
- Tone guardrails: reachability, not danger. No missing-child imagery, police, sirens or crying parents. Dependency, never danger. "The grandmother is never the joke." Older people are capable ("perfectly capable of driving anywhere she likes").

## Real customer language (use this; it's gold)

### Post-purchase survey (1,144 responses, Jan–Mar 2026; 64% found PETL via Meta)
- **Hopes (Q2, n=320):** convenience/ease 30%, travel 17%, portability 14%, multi-device 13%, reliability 12%, MagSafe/Apple 6%, emergency 5%.
- **Why PETL (Q3, n=236):** design/circular 27%, size 21%, saw the ad and liked it, multi-device, Apple compatibility.
- **Objections (Q4, n=174):** **price 47%**, trust/skepticism 10% ("not on Amazon", "FB ads are sketchy", "may be a trick", "never heard of this brand"), quality, wanting more colours, "only 3000mAh", shipping cost.
- Colour as a purchase blocker, verbatim: "Cost, limited colors" · "Color options" · "The color choices" · "Deciding color option" · "Was going to purchase the 2 pack, but didn't see an option to choose the colors" · "Ability to get different colors when ordering 2 for the discount"
- Battery desires, verbatim: "More battery time, especially in an emergency situation" · "Extended battery life out of my iPhone" · "Longer battery usage" · "More battery, ability to charge Apple Watch"
- Feb 2026: "More color choices would be nice. I bought your two neutral colors. Would love to see orange and green options." (Cosmic Orange and Sage now exist.)
- **No real customer has asked for gold or pink** in any survey answer.
- 65+ survey language: "no more rummaging through drawers looking for the right cable", "less cables", "one place".
