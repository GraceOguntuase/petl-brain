The core rule from the dojo: copy what's already working as closely as you can, and let the
data pick the formats. Everything below came out of the Icy Bear thread (Oct 2026). It's
applied here to PETL (the MagSafe battery pack) and NOBL T ravel (the all-in-one carry-
on).
1. Swipe what's working, and swipe it closely
Rip the winning ad almost shot for shot. Change only the product, the avatar and the
reason why . Search in this order: your own niche, then adjacent niches, then brands selling
to the same ICP even if the product is different.

2. Formats vs concepts
A format is the vehicle. A concept is what goes inside it. Most of what we call formats are
really concepts, so we miss whole vehicles.
Formats: skeletons, Pixar-style stories, cartoon characters, street interviews, skits,
follow-my-journey , whitelisted influencer , native pages.
Concepts/structures: 3 reasons why , testimonial, transformation timeline, us vs them,
myth-bust.
Keep a running list of formats that are scaling across the market (ask Iason for his). Each
batch, check which ones PETL and NOBL haven't tried yet.

3. Let the data make the case to the client
When a client pushes a format that isn't working, answer with numbers, not opinions.
1. Pull the last 3 months and split spend and ROAS by format and by who's on screen.
2. Answer two questions:
a. Outside our usual formats, where are we most likely to find a winner?
b. For the format the client wants, is there a pattern in why it's f ailed?
3. T ake a plan built on that to the client.
4. If they still insist, negotiate it down to the smallest share of the batch. Revisit every 3
months.
NOBL: it's a newer brand and retention matters, so start this habit now . A monthly "here's
what the data says" slide builds trust f ast.
4. Mine your own winners for the pattern
The top 10 ads usually tell one story . Find it, then find a brand doing that same pattern
better and rip them.
At Icy Bear , the top 10 shared four things:
An authority f ace (founder or influencer).

Only the top 10 matter for this. Mid and low performers add noise, not direction.
5. Real customers on camera
Book a quick call with a real reviewer and get their story . Write a script from their own
words, then have them film it sitting and talking to camera. T -Man says this has worked
really well for him.
PETL: reviewers with a specific moment, like the phone dying at a gig or the battery
saving them at an airport.
NOBL: reviewers with a story , like a bag surviving a dozen flights, or never checking a
bag again.

9. Check the funnel before blaming the creative
If T OF ads aren't converting, check where they land before writing them off .
Do T OF and BOF ads go to the same page? A page built for buyers can lose cold traffic.
Is tracking working on the T OF landing page?
Is the funnel built around one f ace (celebrity , founder)? That can drag down every non-
founder ad.
10. Don't force AI formats
AI formats (skeletons, cartoons, Pixar stories) are scaling hard for some brands, but not
every audience responds. T est one per batch at most, and rip a proven one closely .
