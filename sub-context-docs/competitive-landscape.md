# Competitive landscape - PETL

## How this map was built

PETL sells a round, aluminum and glass, MagSafe-compatible battery pack in two sizes: the original at $64.95 with 3,000mAh and the PETL 2 Plus at $89.95 with 5,000mAh. One disc charges an iPhone, and the AirPods and Apple Watch can rest on top of it. That product shape decides who it really competes with. On specs per dollar it loses to almost everyone. On size, look, and the three Apple devices on one disc, it competes with a much smaller set.

The map pulls from five places. The first is the 2026-10-27 category ads pull, which walked the Meta Ad Library for PETL's own page plus the keyword searches "magsafe battery pack" and "power bank iphone," all US, active, sorted by impressions, and used AdWhispr for run lengths. The second is the 2026-10-27 audience and market pull, which read Best Buy and Amazon listings, Engadget, ThirstyBear, Tom's Guide and MacRumors coverage, and Trustpilot. The third is NSY's internal record of which rivals the team and client have talked about and named in ads. The fourth is two YouTube reviews of Apple's battery packs that Grace saved into the PETL project in May 2026. The fifth is a live top-up run today through the Meta Ad Library API and AdWhispr, which confirmed The Ridge's push and surfaced a new entrant, Kuxiu.

What's missing matters. There is no spend data for any rival, so every "winner" read rests on run length and Meta's impression sort, per the public library method. Spoken hooks inside competitor videos could not be read from library text, so video reads stop at copy, length, and thumbnail. Amazon and Best Buy individual reviews would not load, so rival review language is thin. TikTok paid ads for the US were out of reach because the TikTok ads tool covers the EEA only. Parker MCP is not connected for this brand, so no Parker competitor ad pull or past Parker chat history went into this.

A note on the field's shape. The category is huge in count and tiny in what matters to PETL. One Meta keyword search for "magsafe power bank" returned an estimated 717 active US ads today, and much of the top of that list is unrelated drama apps, phone cases, and drop-shipped gadgets. Raw presence is not the read. The read is who sells the same promise to the same Apple owner at a nearby price, and who sets the frame that PETL's buyer compares against.

The field sorts into four kinds of rival, and the deep-audit set below covers one of each:

- **First-party.** Apple, whose own MagSafe pack is the reference point PETL's best ads were built around.
- **Mass spec leaders.** Anker first, with Ugreen, INIU, Baseus and Energizer behind it. They win on capacity, Qi2 speed and price.
- **Design-led DTC multi-device.** The Ridge, now pushing a 5-in-1 bank with an Apple Watch charger through MKBHD.
- **Commodity marketplace banks.** The $8 to $20 magnetic packs on Amazon and TikTok Shop, which are the price anchor in the customer's head.

### 1. Apple, MagSafe Battery Pack and iPhone Air MagSafe Battery - slated for deep audit

**Why it's direct.** Apple is the only rival that owns the Apple-native story PETL sells against, and PETL's biggest account franchise was built on it. Inferred, resting on the internal record below.

**What Apple sells.** Two products matter. The original MagSafe Battery Pack launched in 2021 at $99 with 1,460mAh and 5W output, then was discontinued. Those specs are stated in NSY's PETL25 and PETL48 briefs and in a YouTube long-term review Grace saved on May 29, 2026, in which the reviewer says it "has a capacity of 1460 milliamp" and "can charge only at 5 wats." Grace's May 26 note, citing Anker and EcoFlow pages, says Apple retired it because its Lightning port "conflicted with the shift to USB-C across the iPhone 15 lineup." That's stated, from secondhand pages. NSY briefs date the discontinuation to 2023. The newer iPhone Air MagSafe Battery launched at $99 with about 3,149mAh and up to 12W wireless. It's "created exclusively for iPhone Air" and was discounted to $59 on Amazon in May 2026. All of that is stated, from MacRumors on 2026-05-27, MobileSyrup and PhoneArena.

**What reviewers say about it.** The second saved YouTube review, "Watch Before You Buy Apple's New MagSafe Battery...," shows the Air battery on a 16 Pro Max with "an obnoxious gap" and says "it's not going to stay on securely either." It calls it "one of the most frustrating Apple accessories of forever" and notes it skips the "Qi 2.2 25-watt charging standard." The reviewer still sees demand for a first-party pack: "People want an Apple first party MagSafe battery." He then recommends Anker's Ultra Slim 10K, Anker's 5K and a Ugreen Qi2.2 pack instead. That's stated, from the transcript at youtube.com/watch?v=pMLzKy3KvC4. Tom's Guide, via search, says the Air battery "doesn't seem all that practical, especially for the $99 price tag." A 2026-03-16 Appleosophy retest of the original pack found it charged an iPhone 15 Plus "only about two percent over 30–45 minutes, with a case on." Both are stated, via search summary.

**What Apple runs in ads.** Nothing was seen in the Meta keyword results. Apple is a market competitor and a creative non-entity, which is its own finding. That absence is data-limited, because keyword searches may miss Apple's own placements.

**How PETL's team has already met it.** This is the most worked competitor in the account. PETL25 "Apple Discontinued Theirs" framed PETL as the successor to Apple's $99 pack and was called "the account's single biggest engine" in the PETL39 hypothesis in May 2026. That's stated. Its iterations followed: PETL39, PETL42 stress test, PETL43 "Apple Brought It Back For One Phone," PETL45 to PETL47 statics, PETL68 Claire, and the client's own ROG710 unboxing with the hook "Apple actually made a battery pack for this. Then they quietly killed it in 2023." That last one posted 7.1 ROAS on $49 spend, per the 2026-07-15 Fathom summary. That's stated, and the spend is tiny. The same record holds a direct-confrontation failure: "Apple confrontation reliably fails in this account (0 purchases on $185 spend) because of loyalty," from the PETL77 hypothesis, stated. The working rule the team landed on is successor framing yes, attack no. That's inferred from the two results side by side. By mid-October the franchise was fading: PETL25 H3 was flagged PAUSE at 1.28x over 7 days against a 2.23x bench, per PETL Analytics for the week ending 2026-10-18. That's stated. And the "half the price of Apple's $99 pack" line no longer holds on one unit at $64.95. Briefs now say to compare the 3-pack at $48.72 each instead, per Notion PETL68. That's stated.

**Kind of threat.** Frame-setter rather than share-taker. Apple decides what "the real one" looks like to a loyal Apple buyer. Its $59 Air battery discount moves the price anchor under the original PETL, and any future universal Apple pack would collapse the successor story overnight. Inferred.

**A tension the deep audit should settle.** NSY's PETL43 brief says the Air battery "can't wirelessly charge Watch/AirPods." The audience pull cites PhoneArena saying it "also charges AirPods and Apple Watch," and the YouTube reviewer says it charges AirPods through its USB-C port. These are not fully the same claim. Any comparison ad needs the exact, current answer before it ships. Data-limited.

## Open loops

**1. Kuxiu's burst against PETL's buyer.** Kuxiu launched about 39 US ads in 12 days pitching a 3-in-1 aluminum MagSafe bank, with Cosmic Orange and Burgundy colorways and Apple-adjacent lines like "Your iPhone should come with this." Why it's a loop: if the audiences overlap, PETL's three-device ads now face a direct look-alike in the same auction going into Q4. That changes whether PETL leans on "three devices" or on shape and size. Territory: personas.

**2. Why the Apple successor story faded.** PETL25 "Apple Discontinued Theirs" was the account's biggest engine in May, but by the week ending 2026-10-18 its H3 ad was flagged PAUSE at 1.28x against a 2.23x bench. Over the same months, Apple's Air battery dropped to $59 and PETL's price rose to $64.95. Pull: tension. The internal record says the story works, and the latest number says it doesn't, while two outside facts moved underneath it. Question: What changed for buyers between the months the Apple successor story carried the account and October, when it stopped converting? Why it's a loop: it decides whether the franchise needs a new comparison anchor or should be retired, and that's the account's most worked competitive angle. Territory: messaging.

**3. No outside reviewer behind PETL.** The Ridge has MKBHD, Clicks has jeffgadway, and Latercase has Brian's Tech. Category buyers lean on reviewer roundups from Engadget, Tom's Guide and YouTube, and none of the roundups read for this map names PETL. PETL's ranking winners use NSY creators playing reviewers, and the site's "Expert Tech Reviewer" quotes appear to come from partner creators. Pull: gap. The field runs on third-party reviewer trust, and PETL has none on record. Question: Why hasn't an independent tech reviewer covered PETL yet? Why it's a loop: if the gap is reach or seeding rather than product, the answer opens a creator lane that rivals are already using against PETL. If reviewers have looked and passed, that's a product signal the brand needs to hear. Territory: creators and talent.

**4. What "not on Amazon" costs.** Amazon is where most units in the category sell, and 62% of products there sit at $20 to $50. PETL isn't sold there, and 10% of the what-almost-stopped-you answers in the survey cited skepticism or "not on Amazon." Pull: pattern. The Amazon comparison shows up in PETL's own winning scripts, customer stories and survey answers. Question: How many PETL shoppers look for it on Amazon before they buy, and what do they end up doing when it isn't there? Why it's a loop: it sizes how much of PETL's price objection is really a trust and channel objection. That changes whether comparison creative should target the commodity bank or the missing Amazon listing. Territory: product.
