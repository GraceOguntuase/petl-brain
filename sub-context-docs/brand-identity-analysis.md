# Brand identity analysis — PETL (goPETL)

**How to read this.** This doc is the brand talking about itself. Almost everything here is the brand's own claim, captured faithfully and marked. **Stated** means the brand or a team member says it and nobody has checked it. **Verified** means it was observed directly on the live site on 2026-10-27, or in a dated artifact. For product claims, verified means "verified that PETL shows this," not that the claim is independently true. **Inferred** marks Parker's own read, kept light. **Data-limited** means it can't be resolved from what's here. ## What the brand is, who they are, what they sell

PETL is a small circular battery pack that snaps magnetically onto the back of an iPhone and also charges an Apple Watch and AirPods placed on top of it. The company is goPETL LLC, sold only at gopetl.com. Two battery packs make up the line: the original PETL at $64.95 and the PETL 2 Plus at $89.95, plus two keychain cables. *Verified — live PDPs and footer "© 2026 goPETL LLC," 2026-10-27.* The product detail lives in `website-and-product-audit.md`.

**The brand's own one-line description of itself** is the homepage hero, unchanged since at least January: "World's First Circular MagSafe Compatible Battery Pack." *Verified — homepage, 2026-10-27; same line in the 2026-01-14 Website Copy capture.* The other self-descriptions it leans on, all verified on the live homepage or PDP on 2026-10-27:

- "Replace 3 chargers with 1 PETL." and "One PETL. iPhone, AirPods, Watch — charged."
- "One little charger. A lot less to carry."
- "Most magsafe power banks add battery. PETL rethinks the whole charging experience."
- "Made to match your Apple everything."
- "the world's only circular top-up battery" (homepage)

**How it frames itself against what buyers already know.** The enemy in the brand's own copy is "bulky battery bricks," "cheap plastic," and carrying three chargers for three devices. The brand calls itself a "top-up battery," not a big refill, and a brand blog post says the pack "is not the highest-capacity pack on the market, and that is by design." *Stated — brand blog via external-research-2026-10.md; "top-up" verified on homepage 2026-10-27.* The onboarding form puts the same idea in the founder's words: most power banks "force users to choose between battery life and comfort. PETL eliminates this tradeoff." *Stated — onboarding form, 2026-01-12.*

**Parker's read, marked as such:** the brand's picture of itself is sharp and consistent. Across the January site copy, the onboarding form, the About page and today's homepage, it says the same three things every time: it's round and small enough to carry, it charges all three Apple devices, and it's built like a premium object. *Inferred — from comparing those four sources.* Where the picture gets fuzzy is the proof and the fine print, covered under claim substantiation below, not the positioning.

## Brand guidelines

**There is no written brand guide.** No palette, font, logo usage or tone document exists in any source read. A Drive folder called BRAND ASSETS is linked from the onboarding form but holds no written rules that anyone has captured. *Data-limited — internal digest §8 item 17; onboarding form link not opened.* That blank matters: the rules below are scattered across one onboarding form, Rakesh's Slack and call comments, and NSY's own briefs.

The rules split into two kinds, and the difference matters for who can change them.

**Rules the brand itself set (firm, from Rakesh or the onboarding form):**

1. **No Apple endorsement language.** "Do not imply or state: 'Apple-approved' • 'Apple-certified' • 'Made for Apple' (unless officially licensed) • 'Official Apple accessory'. You may say 'compatible with iPhone, Apple Watch, and AirPods' only in a factual compatibility context." *Stated — onboarding form, 2026-01-12. Framed as a firm rule.*
2. **Keep it branded, clean, elegant; avoid gimmicky.** *Stated — onboarding form and call, January 2026. Framed as a standard.*
3. **Real UGC for testimonial angles, not AI.** "We need to do this angle with real UGC not AI." *Stated — Rakesh, Slack 2026-09-10.* AI voiceover over B-roll is accepted for top-of-funnel message tests. *Stated — Fathom 2026-10-09.*
4. **Ads must match the live offer.** "please check our prices and offer have changed, make sure new ads match the present offer." *Stated — Rakesh, Slack 2026-08-25. Firm.*
5. **Competitors may be named:** Apple, Anker, Belkin, Mophie. *Stated — PETL48 brief hypothesis; the explicit approval message was never located. Data-limited on scope.*
6. **"Tariffs" may be named as the reason prices rise; no political figures.** *Stated — docs/01, confirmed by Rakesh per that doc.*
7. **INTL ad names must never carry "NSY."** *Stated — Rakesh, Slack 2026-06-10. Operational, firm.*
8. **Occasion wording like "Pre-Black Friday" needs Rakesh's sign-off before launch.** *Stated — PETL112 brief, 2026-10-06.*

**Rules NSY built and the brand has accepted in practice (agency rules, firm inside NSY briefs):**

- Say "MagSafe-compatible." No Apple logos or marks; brand names in text only. *Stated — PETL68 and PETL51 briefs.*
- Always "PETL 2 Plus," never "PETL Plus." Upgrade framing only: "Never make the original PETL look weak." *Stated — PETL124 brief.* Note the site's own bundle selector says "PETL PLUS" and the nav says "PETL PLUS." *Verified 2026-10-27.* So the brand's own site breaks this naming rule.
- Never call PETL a "thing" or a "circle." *Stated — PETL104 brief.*
- Say "iPhone," not "phone," for compatibility; say "snap it on." *Stated — docs/04.*
- "Qi2": say "meets the Qi2 standard," never "Qi2 certified." *Stated — docs/01.* See substantiation: the live site never mentions Qi2 at all.
- No fabricated social proof or fake branded AI screenshots in final production. *Stated — docs/04 §H.*
- Urgency must be anchored to something real; no invented deadlines. *Stated — PETL92 hypothesis and docs/01.*
- Profanity: soft or censored only. *Inferred from Slack 2026-10-16.*
- Creators film raw, vertical, no branded clothing, cinematic mode preferred, emphasize the magnetic snap sound. *Stated — PETL68 brief.*

## Compliance and legal guardrails

Recorded as stated. Nothing here is a legal opinion.

**What the brand itself says it can't do (firm):**
- Imply Apple affiliation, approval, certification or official status. The only allowed Apple wording is factual compatibility. *Stated — onboarding form, 2026-01-12.* The site backs this with "PETL is not affiliated with or endorsed by Apple Inc." *Verified — footer.*
- Run ads that don't match the live price and offer. *Stated — Rakesh, Slack 2026-08-25.*
- Use AI people for testimonial angles. *Stated — Rakesh, Slack 2026-09-10.*
- Name political figures. *Stated — docs/01.*

**What the agency added and enforces (firm inside briefs):** no fake social proof or branded AI interface screenshots in final production, citing Meta deceptive-content and FTC endorsement risk; urgency anchored only to real events; no countdowns; competitor logos blurred or shown as text only. *Stated — docs/04 §H and docs/01.*

**Legal facts the brand displays that a script team needs to know:**
- A California Prop 65 lead warning appears on the PETL PDP. *Verified — PDP, 2026-10-27.*
- The "10+ hours" claims carry a footnote limiting them to internal iPhone 16 testing under ideal conditions. *Verified.*
- The return policy carries limits that the PDP headline does not. *Verified — refund policy, 2026-10-27.* The policy also says it applies only to purchases made after it was updated, without a date. *Verified.*

**Flagged to confirm with the brand, standing not settled by anything the brand provides:**
1. Whether "world's first circular" can be backed with evidence.
2. Whether a patent application exists, and which word the brand wants: "patent pending" or "patented." The live homepage uses both today.
3. Whether PETL has any MFi or MagSafe licensing. The onboarding rule says "Made for Apple" is banned "unless officially licensed," and the homepage says "Made for iPhone Pro & Pro Max." No license was ever mentioned in any source.
4. Which certification documents exist, especially UL, given the 2025 Anker power bank recalls that shape category trust.
5. How the Apple Watch actually charges, and on which Watch models. Every three-device ad rests on it.
6. Which customer or unit count is true today.
7. Whether "no questions asked" can stay on the PDP next to a $5 return fee.

Observation: the onboarding form describes the buyer as a design-conscious minimalist drawn to "premium, understated brands," and the site never names an age or a gift buyer. The founder's own read of the ad data is "older demographics work better," and the team cites women 65+ as the largest segment.
Pull: **Tension.** The brand's written persona and the founder's own data read point at two different people.
Question: Who does PETL's identity actually belong to, the minimalist the brand describes or the older gift buyer its own ads are reaching?
Justification: if the real buyer is the older gift buyer, the brand's whole register, site copy, colorway story and "clean and elegant" standard may be tuned for a customer it mostly doesn't sell to.
Territory: Personas. *Routes partly to the brand.*

**2. The "world's first" identity rests on a claim no source backs.**
Observation: "World's First Circular" is the hero line, the PDP title and a live ad line, and the brand offers no evidence for it. The homepage also says both "patent pending" and "patented" today.
Pull: **Gap.** The single most repeated identity claim has nothing behind it in any source.
Question: What evidence does PETL hold that it was the first circular MagSafe-compatible battery and that the design is protected?
Justification: if the brand can't back "first" or "patented," the identity has to be carried by something it can prove, and the line the account has used most would need replacing.
Territory: Messaging. *Brand-only — route to Rakesh.*

**3. The brand sells quiet luxury on a loud page.**
Observation: Rakesh wants ads "clean and elegant" and says gimmicky ads failed, yet the live page stacks four discount mechanics, a "pay full price" shame button, "sells out fast," and "Sold out 7 times" around the premium copy.
Pull: **Tension.** The stated identity and the page it lives on send opposite signals.
Question: How much does the discount and urgency layer change how buyers see PETL's premium price?
Justification: if the offer layer is what makes a $64.95 price feel fair, it belongs in the identity; if it erodes the premium read, it is costing the brand the very thing it says it is.
Territory: Messaging.
