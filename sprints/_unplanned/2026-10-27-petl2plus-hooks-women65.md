# PETL 2 Plus hooks, women 65+ (final, 2026-10-27)

Task: 3 hooks for the PETL 2 Plus aimed at women 65+. Meta (Facebook feed first). Brain: ~/Documents/GitHub/petl-brain.
ICP: the No-Fuss Family Keeper (personas/personas-profile.md). Women 65+ = 30.8% of purchases at $63.21 CPA, last 90 days (verified, Meta pull 2026-10-27).
Strategy check: `strategy/` and `idea-bank/entries/` exist but are empty, so no committed roadmap or idea entry frames this set.
All overlays and spoken lines below are written script lines for the creator, not customer quotes, except the Hook 3 quote card, which is a real survey answer.

## Hook 1 — Permission / Zone 1 with an explicit age callout, as a QVC-style demo — "tech made simple" relief for the 65+ woman who isn't a gadget person
- Text overlay (0–1s): "Over 65 and not a gadget person?" (large, high-contrast)
- Spoken (~1.5–4s): "You don't have to be good with gadgets for this one. This little circular power bank, it just snaps right onto the back of your iPhone. That's it." (Body, next beat: "And later on, you just set your AirPods or your Apple Watch on top.")
- Visual: Kitchen table, morning light, a plain shot she could have filmed herself. A woman around 70, reading glasses on, holds her iPhone in a MagSafe case, bracing a little like a setup screen is coming. At about 1 second she brings the PETL 2 Plus to the back of the case and it clicks on by itself; the charging ring lights. She lowers her glasses, small surprised smile, and holds it there. In the body, a separate beat: the PETL off the phone, lying on the table, and she sets her AirPods case on top; then another separate beat for her Apple Watch. The iPhone and another device are never on the PETL together. No Apple logos in frame.
- Sound: the magnetic click is the first sound, alone, then warm chill lofi under her voice. No swell.
- Vibe: calm, ordinary, her own kitchen. Slow holds, cuts every 3 to 5 seconds.
- Emotion: relief that this one is easy (Zone 1). Tag: Untapped opportunity. This runs the client's own unlaunched test, "age-appropriate female unboxing with all three devices charging in the first 3 seconds" (brand-lens.md line 170; monthly report line 153 says no such ad is live), staged one device per beat (all three appear within the first scenes, never charging together) so it stays inside the "set on top" claim and off "at the same time."
- Why it fits: the survey's top hope is ease (89 of 320 answers), and the team says many older buyers "don't know the word MagSafe," so a hook that drops the jargon and shows the click meets her where she is. The older-audience method names "tech made simple" as a core lever and asks for an explicit age anchor, which the overlay gives Meta and her at once.
- Sources: "Ease of use for an elderly woman" (#GP7130, 2026-03-12); "Looks easy to use" (#GP5415, 2026-02-17); 02-audience doc line 24. Snap-on, iPhone 12 and newer, and "set on top" are safe claims (website-and-product-audit.md claim table). The iPhone 12+ line belongs on screen in the body.

## Hook 2 — Demographic / identity callout — the grandma who keeps the whole family charged, quiet pride in being ready
- Text overlay, staged: beat 1 (0–1s) "Grandma always has a charger." Beat 2 (~2s) "Here's mine."
- Spoken (~2–4s): "Every family's got that one person who always has a charger on them. And in ours, that's me."
- Visual: A simple, close frame at the family table so the text can be read: her purse on the chair beside her. A teenage grandson's hand slides his iPhone (in a MagSafe case) across the tablecloth toward her, screen showing a low battery. She doesn't look up from her coffee, reaches into the purse, pulls out a PETL 2 Plus and clicks it onto the back of his phone. At about 3 seconds the camera finds her face, a little smug, looking at us.
- Sound: soft table chatter, then the magnetic click, then her line. Lofi comes in under it.
- Vibe: warm, a bit funny, real family.
- Emotion: being ready for me and mine, a calm pride (Zone 1, a mild Zone 3 low-battery beat that's played for a smile, not panic). Tag: Safe bet, on the family and gifting lane (brand-lens.md line 144: gifting to and within family is a core lane; Rakesh: "older demographics work better").
- Why it fits: her own words put the family in the purchase ("Portable charger for me & family"), and "grandma" is an identity she claims herself, which the demographic process asks for. Defaulting older scripts to grandma and women is the team's own rule.
- Sources: "Portable charger for me & family" (#GP5994, 2026-02-26); "Security knowing I will always have a charger with me." (#GP8077, 2026-03-22); brand-lens.md line 143.

## Hook 3 — Comment response — the first-time trust check, answered by a woman her age
- Text overlay, staged: beat 1 (0–1s) "Is PETL legit?" Beat 2 (~1.5s) a quote card styled like a comment: "Wasn't sure if this was going to be a legit product." with the label "a PETL customer". (The quote is a survey answer, #GP6117, shown in full.)
- Spoken (~2–4s): "Yeah, I wasn't sure it was legit either. So here's what I checked before I bought my PETL 2 Plus."
- Visual: The woman, late 60s, on her couch, the quote card floating above her (green-screen style). She points up at it and nods, then holds the PETL 2 Plus box up to the camera. Slow push-in on her face.
- Sound: her voice first, no music for the first beat, then soft lofi.
- Vibe: honest, neighborly, a friend telling you straight.
- Emotion: guarded hope at the moment of buying. Tag: Untapped opportunity (no top-30 ad is briefed to answer "is this real"). Best on warm and retargeting audiences, since trust is a close-stage feeling for this buyer.
- Why it fits: 23 of 222 objection answers are trust doubts from first-time buyers who found PETL in their feed. A woman her own age checking the proof is age-matched social proof, and the body's high review count is the trusted-source frame the older-audience method asks for.
- Body payoffs that are safe: "4.8 stars from over 1,400 reviews," "Try it risk-free for 30 days," "ships same day if you order before noon Eastern." Creator must be a real owner, with paid-partnership disclosure.

## Brand Context Applied
- What I used: the No-Fuss Family Keeper persona, the survey's ease, family and trust language, the client's female-65+ test list, the older-audience method (explicit callouts, big text, slow holds, tech made simple, age-matched proof), and the PETL claim table.
- What I avoided: "PETL 2 Plus" always in full; no Apple logos, MagSafe case on every iPhone; no "full charge" in hooks (the Plus can say "nearly a full charge on an iPhone 16 Pro" in the body); no "at the same time"; no "full refund" or "no questions asked"; no in-flight shots; no panic opens. American English.
- Why this fits: account-wide, clicks grew faster than purchases this month and each funnel step converted slightly worse (verified, Meta pull 2026-10-27), so hooks that qualify the right buyer plainly, by age, by "grandma," or by the trust question, and show the product early matter more (inferred).
